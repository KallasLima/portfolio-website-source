About me

I'm a software engineer who has worked as both a Staff Engineer and an Engineering Manager. My work includes building products from the beginning, changing systems that people already depend on, and leading the engineers responsible for them.

I've worked on business software, financial systems, mobile applications, production AI features and software used in physical retail operations. Depending on the problem, my responsibility has covered the architecture, implementation, infrastructure, team and operation of the product.

Full-stack product engineering

I usually want context before I start changing a system. I want to understand who uses it, how the current behavior works, what the business is trying to change, and where the proposed solution came from.

I'm comfortable following a problem across backend services, web and mobile applications, databases and infrastructure. At Olly Olly, that meant building the foundations of a new software platform while developing the product itself. At CookUnity, it meant moving between customer applications and the systems used to operate Smart Fridges.

I stay involved after release as well. My work has included incident investigation, debugging, code review, release preparation, automated testing and monitoring.

AI & projects

Coding agents · Public work

How I use coding agents

Coding agents are part of my daily work. I use them for research, implementation, tests, review, debugging and documentation. I often have several agents working in parallel while I concentrate on the decisions that need my attention.

The context I give an agent matters. A useful assignment includes the intended outcome, the relevant systems, constraints, supporting material and a way to establish whether the work is complete. I review the proposed approach and the result, question assumptions and redirect the work when it misses the point.

I remain responsible for what is built and what ships. I want evidence that the result works in its intended setting, including the user flow when the change affects one. A successful command or a passing test can answer a specific question; I still need to understand what it establishes about the actual product.

About this project

Design · Engineering · Human and AI collaboration

I wanted this website to give more context than a résumé could: the problems I've worked on, the decisions I've made and how I approach engineering. Building the cockpit became another example of that process.

Finding a direction

The first concepts were too plain. I wanted more color, more technology and something worth exploring. I tried several directions, including a pixel world and a portable computer, before settling on a spaceship cockpit.

The early spaceship images went too far in the other direction. They suggested a large game environment that would be difficult to build and distract from the writing. The useful idea was smaller: a cockpit, a view into space and a set of displays where the content stays readable.

From generated images to working materials

AI image generation helped me compare designs before committing to an implementation. I reacted to the results, chose details and rejected others: brighter lights, a darker cabin, silver display rims and fewer decorative controls that looked clickable.

I instructed the AI to build the cockpit and display models in Blender, then directed revisions to materials, texture, lighting and proportions. Later, I sent screenshots of the working website back through image editing to explore a more detailed finish. The approved result became a reference for changes to the actual site, including generated cabin artwork prepared as runtime assets.

Making the scene behave

The website combines layered space artwork, rendered cabin surfaces and live display geometry. The writing is HTML, so it stays selectable and scrollable as the displays move. The screens, their text, reflections and shadows have to agree about where each display is.

That took repeated corrections. Side hinges appeared to slide. Tilted edges looked jagged. Reflections made the glass look cracked. Shadows needed to follow the console's different heights. I could see when something felt wrong, even when I didn't yet know the graphics term for it.

Travel needed the same attention. Planets drifting sideways felt like objects being rearranged. I changed the movement to suggest approaching them, passing them and pulling away across much larger distances.

Keeping it usable

More detail also brought performance problems. I measured the animation, reused prepared artwork and lighting, and kept expensive rendering for the parts that actually change. Fixed cabin lighting can be baked into an image; a folding display still needs its pose, glass and shadows to respond.

The text remained the priority. I adjusted the reading area, made vertical scrolling read the article, and kept chapter navigation separate. On phones, the side displays disappear so the main display and the view outside have enough room.

Working with AI

I directed the project through references, questions, choices and hands-on feedback. The coding agent investigated problems, proposed approaches, generated iterations, implemented changes and checked the result.

The final view is an artist's impression inspired by the Local Group, our neighborhood of galaxies. Positions and distances are compressed for this composition.
